## Turn the search into a manageable process

Looking for a job can feel like a huge task if you start by sending applications without a plan. First, define two or three types of roles that interest you and note which conditions matter to you, such as the tasks, work arrangement, or location. You do not have to choose a career forever: the goal is to pick an initial focus that you can review later.

Read several recent job postings for those roles and extract recurring requirements. Sort them into three groups: “I have done this,” “I have practiced this in a project or training,” and “I have not tried this yet.” This distinction helps you adapt your CV without presenting practice as work experience. If a posting asks for a technology you have only studied, you can describe the specific exercise and the level of independence with which you completed it.

## Prepare a base version and adapt each application

Keep a base CV with accurate information and a clear projects section. For each posting, adjust the summary and highlight the most relevant evidence first. You do not need to rewrite everything: a person should be able to see the connection between what the posting asks for and what you can demonstrate.

Describe projects with precise verbs: “implemented a filter,” “wrote tests for…,” or “documented the installation steps.” Include tools you actually used and explain the context. Avoid labels such as “expert” or impact metrics you cannot substantiate. If you have not yet worked in a development role, present projects as personal or training projects, not jobs.

Use previous projects as evidence: explain what need you addressed, what part you implemented, what tests you ran, and what limitation remains. If you used AI during development, you can describe the task it helped with and how you reviewed its proposal. Distinguish carefully between a local demo and a service used by customers, and between manual verification and a test suite; those differences make your profile more credible and help you discuss specific technical decisions.

InfoJobs' official help pages provide instructions for editing CV fields and creating another CV or attaching an external one. Consult those pages before making changes: option names may differ between the website and the app. Also review dates, links, the introductory text, and visibility settings before saving. Include only information you want to share in the process.

## Write a specific application

Before applying, reread the posting and decide whether the role and its tasks interest you. In a short message, you can mention the role, relevant evidence, and why you would like to discuss the work. For example: “I’m interested in the junior web development role. In a training project, I built a page with form validation and documented how to test it. I’d be glad to explain more about how I approached testing.” Replace the example with facts about your own experience; do not claim to have used a tool professionally if you only tried it in an exercise.

If an application includes questions, answer directly and consistently with your CV. If you do not meet a requirement, do not hide that behind an ambiguous phrase: point to related experience you do have or say which part you have not practiced yet. Hiring depends on each process and many factors; a clear application does not guarantee an interview or a job, but it does make your profile easier to understand.

Keep a simple record in a local document: date, role, link to the posting, CV version, and known status. Also note a next action, such as reviewing the description or preparing an example for a conversation. Avoid recording unnecessary personal data. Tracking helps you avoid confusing applications and review which types of roles best match your evidence.

## Verifiable practice

Choose an active job posting that interests you. Copy five requirements into a personal table and write one piece of evidence next to each: a project, task, course, or real experience. Separately mark what you cannot yet demonstrate and choose a small step to practice it. Then adapt three lines of your summary and write four sentences to introduce your application.

Before saving or sending, check that every statement in the text is backed by something you could explain or show. Consult InfoJobs' official articles about editing a CV and creating or attaching another one if you need to update your profile. You will finish with a reviewed version and a record of what you changed, without assuming that a platform or a single application will resolve the job search.
