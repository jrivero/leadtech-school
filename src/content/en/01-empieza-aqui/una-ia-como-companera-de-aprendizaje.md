## AI as a Tutor, Not a Substitute

An AI assistant can offer explanations, examples, questions, and hints, but a fluent answer is not guaranteed to be correct or to fit your exercise. To learn, use it as a conversation partner: you explain what you already know, it helps locate the question, and you check the answer. Do not delegate your learning goal or the judgment of whether a solution works.

A useful prompt includes four elements: your level, the context, where you got stuck, and the kind of help you want. For example: “I’m just starting with Python. I know that a function groups instructions, but I don’t understand what `return` gives back. Don’t give me the full solution: ask me a question, then review my explanation.” This lets you request a specific hint instead of accepting a block of code you do not yet understand.

## A Learning Dialogue

Suppose you wrote an addition function and are not sure whether it should use `print` or `return`. First write down your hypothesis. Then ask the AI for two minimal versions and an explanation of who can use the result in each case. Before copying anything, try to explain it in your own words: “`print` displays something now; `return` passes a value back to whoever called the function.”

Next, test the idea in a new file. Run the function with small numbers, save the result in a variable, and display it afterward. If the explanation and the execution do not match, share the error message and ask what it means, without sending passwords, keys, or personal data. The answer may contain errors: compare it with the language documentation and keep the example you verified.

Some assistants’ study modes are designed to guide learners with questions, explanations in stages, and checks for understanding. Even if you use another tool or an ordinary conversation, you can explicitly request that style. The exact feature and its availability vary by product; the learning method does not depend on a commercial feature.

## Step-by-Step Practice

1. Choose an idea you have just studied, such as a variable or a condition.
2. Before opening the AI tool, write a two-sentence explanation and your own example.
3. Ask for a hint, not the answer, and request a follow-up question.
4. Answer without copying. If your answer is wrong, point out what you do not understand and ask for a simpler explanation.
5. Run the suggested example and change one input to see whether the rule still works.
6. Write down what you confirmed, what you corrected, and what you need to check in an official source.

## Check Your Understanding and Know the Limits

The session was useful if you can close the conversation, explain the idea, modify the example, and predict what output should appear. If you cannot, ask for less content, a smaller case, or one hint at a time. If the AI claims something that contradicts the program or its documentation, do not try to rationalize the contradiction: create a minimal example and find out which explanation withstands the test.

Do not share private company code, credentials, or sensitive information. Check your course or workplace rules before using AI on an assignment. When asking for debugging help, include only the smallest necessary snippet, the expected result, and the observed result; remove any data that does not help answer the question.

## Common Mistakes

Do not ask “do the whole exercise for me” if your goal is to practice. Do not confuse a detailed answer with evidence that you understand. Avoid switching topics every time an explanation is difficult: narrow the question and test one example. AI can also invent functions, option names, or citations; verify any instructions before running them.

## Summary

Use AI to get hints, compare ideas, and generate practice; keep prediction, execution, and the final explanation for yourself. Verify important claims in code and reliable sources. The judgment you develop is the outcome of the session.
